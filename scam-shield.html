Scam shield

Got a suspicious call? Speed matters more than anything else. Follow these steps in order.

The first 10 minutes

  1. Hang up. Don't argue and don't confirm any details. If they said they're from your bank or the police, end the call anyway.
  2. If you shared a code, PIN or card number, call your bank now using the number on your card — not a number the caller gave you. Ask them to block the card and freeze transfers.
  3. If you installed an app they asked for, turn on airplane mode and remove it. Change passwords from a different device.
  4. Report it to the hotline for your country (below). Reports in the first hours give the best chance of freezing a transfer.
  5. Warn your family. Scammers often call relatives next using what they learned from you.

Red flags in any language

  • Urgency: "today", "right now", "or your account will be closed".
  • Secrecy: "don't tell your family or the bank teller".
  • Unusual payment: gift cards, crypto, a "safe account", cash handed to a courier.
  • Requests for one-time codes, PINs, CVV or ID numbers.
  • Pressure to install an app or allow screen sharing.
  • A caller ID that looks official — caller IDs can be faked.

Protect an older parent

Phone-scam losses are highest among older people. Agree a family code word, set their phone to silence unknown callers, ask their bank about daily transfer limits, and tell them they can always hang up and call you first.
